📊 Comparison of Adverbs (Rule 273)

Adverbs (mostly of Manner/Quality) are compared like adjectives.

(a) One-syllable adverbs → -er / -est

PositiveComparativeSuperlative
fastfasterfastest
soonsoonersoonest
latelaterlatest
longlongerlongest
hardharderhardest
slowslowerslowest

(b) -ly adverbs → more/less · most/least

PositiveComparativeSuperlative
slowlymore / less slowlymost / least slowly
wiselymore / less wiselymost / least wisely
rapidlymore / less rapidlymost / least rapidly
quicklymore / less quicklymost / least quickly
swiftlymore / less swiftlymost / least swiftly
earlyearlierearliest

(c) Irregular Comparison

PositiveComparativeSuperlative
well / goodbetterbest
littlelessleast
muchmoremost
forthfurtherfurthest

(d) No comparison

Many adverbs have no comparative form: now, then, here, there, once, already, almost.

Important: Both Adjectives and Adverbs can be compared. Distinguish by function — Adjectives compare Nouns; Adverbs compare verbs, adjectives or other adverbs.
